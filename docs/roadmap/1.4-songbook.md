# 1.4 — The Songbook

*Released as 1.4.0 on 2026-11-18. As built:*

- *28 tunes and 96 versions, written in one pass rather than in reviewed batches; versions were kept only where the
  recording is well documented (three to five per tune, not four to eight). The remaining tunes of the first list
  (Manteca, Misty, So What, Giant Steps, Naima, Moanin', Maiden Voyage, Stella by Starlight, Song for My Father,
  Ornithology and others) wait for the next batch.*
- *Versions resolve in `build-tracks.py` under `"artist|title|year"`, with an optional `record` hint (the album
  they're on), so two recordings by one artist don't collide. There's no separate `build-songbook.py` yet: the
  MusicBrainz work check is still to do.*
- *Progression pages (`rhythm-changes`, `blues`) and versions heard in the passport are not done; families only link
  tunes in the book (none of the first 28 are contrafacts of each other).*
- *The iPhone's Explore tab replaced the Styles/Decades switch with an era carousel and rows; `erasMode` remains
  only so old links still land.*

Jazz is the art of playing the same song differently. Every other section of the app is organised by who or when;
this one is organised by *what*: a tune, and the versions that made it matter. Hearing "Body and Soul" in 1939, 1960
and last year, back to back, explains the music faster than any paragraph.

Needs 1.3's `tracks.json` pipeline. It's also where the iPhone's Eras tab becomes **Explore**.

## What people get

- **The Songbook**: about 40 tunes to start, standards and jazz originals.
- **A page per tune**: its story, who wrote it and when, its form in a line, and four to eight landmark versions on a
  timeline, each with a sentence on what to listen for.
- **Play them in order**: one tap queues every version, oldest first.
- **Families**: tunes built on another tune's chords ("Ornithology" on "How High the Moon") link to each other.
- **Tunes everywhere**: a tune's name in any tracklist links to its page; artist pages list the tunes they wrote and
  the versions they own.
- **Songbook Radio**: a station per tune, and one that shuffles versions across the whole book.

## Data

### `songbook.json` (new, hand-written, checked by script)

```json
{
  "tunes": [
    {
      "id": "body-and-soul",
      "title": "Body and Soul",
      "composers": ["Johnny Green"],
      "lyricists": ["Edward Heyman", "Robert Sour", "Frank Eyton"],
      "year": 1930,
      "origin": "Popular song",
      "form": "AABA, 32 bars",
      "summary": "Two paragraphs: where it came from, and what makes players keep coming back to it.",
      "builtOn": null,
      "aliases": [],
      "versions": [
        {
          "artist": "coleman-hawkins", "artistName": "Coleman Hawkins", "year": 1939,
          "album": "hawkins-body-and-soul",
          "note": "What to listen for, in one or two sentences.",
          "landmark": true
        }
      ]
    }
  ]
}
```

- `origin` is one of: Popular song, Show tune, Film song, Blues, Jazz original, Latin standard.
- `composers` may hold one of our artist IDs when the composer has a page (Monk, Ellington, Shorter); the app shows a
  link then, plain text otherwise.
- `builtOn` is another tune's ID (a contrafact) or a named progression (`"rhythm-changes"`, `"blues"`), which get
  short pages of their own listing everything built on them.
- `aliases` are other titles the same tune appears under in tracklists.
- A version's `artist` is one of our IDs or `null`; `album` is a Crate album when the version is on one. Its track is
  resolved by `build-tracks.py` under the key `"<artistName>|<title>"`, same as every other named track, with the
  version's year as a hint to pick the right recording when an artist cut the tune more than once.
- `landmark` marks the one or two versions to play if you only play one.

### Which tunes

Forty to start, chosen for range rather than a canon: popular songs jazz adopted, blues, originals by players already
in the app, and Latin standards. A first list to react to, not a final one: *St. Louis Blues, Stardust, Body and Soul,
I Got Rhythm, Summertime, Caravan, Cherokee, All the Things You Are, How High the Moon, Take the "A" Train, A Night in
Tunisia, 'Round Midnight, Manteca, Autumn Leaves, My Funny Valentine, Lush Life, Misty, Blue Monk, Straight No
Chaser, Moanin', So What, My Favorite Things, Giant Steps, Naima, Take Five, Goodbye Pork Pie Hat, Afro Blue,
Watermelon Man, Song for My Father, The Girl from Ipanema, Maiden Voyage, Footprints, Nardis, Lonely Woman, Stella by
Starlight, On Green Dolphin Street, In a Sentimental Mood, Night and Day, Lover Man, Birdland.*

Picking versions is the real editorial work: each tune wants an early statement, the version that changed how it was
played, a singer if it has words, something from outside the US mainstream, and something recent. Where the best
version is by someone not in the app, that's a candidate for [catalog.md](catalog.md).

### `scripts/build-songbook.py`

Checks facts against MusicBrainz's "work" records (the Kind of Blue probe returned composers for every tune):
composers and lyricists match, the year isn't later than the earliest recording, each version's artist recorded
something by that title around that year. It reports disagreements; it doesn't rewrite the file. Then it runs the
track lookup for any version not yet in `tracks.json`.

### Tunes on records

With `tracklists.json` (1.3), the loader builds an index from normalized track title to tune, using `title` and
`aliases`, so tracklist rows can link to tune pages and a tune page can list "Also in the Crate": versions on curated
albums that weren't hand-picked. Titles are ambiguous ("Blues" isn't a tune; two different songs can share a name), so
the index only links exact matches of tunes in the book, and the validator prints every automatic match for a
read-through. A `"notOn": ["album-id"]` list on a tune removes false ones.

### Validator

IDs unique; artist, album and `builtOn` references resolve; each tune has at least three versions with a note, at
least one landmark, and versions in at least two eras; every version resolves in `tracks.json` or is listed as a miss.

```swift
struct Tune: Codable, Identifiable, Hashable { … ; var decadeSpan: ClosedRange<Int> }

    func tune(forTrackTitle title: String) -> Tune?      // the index above

    func versions(by artistID: String) -> [(Tune, TuneVersion)]
    func family(of tune: Tune) -> [Tune]                 // built on it, or on the same thing

```

`Route.tune(String)`, `Route.progression(String)`; `PlayContext.tune(String)`; `Station.Kind.tune(String)` and
`.songbook`. Search results gain tunes (matching title, alias, composer). The passport gains "versions heard" per
tune (a set of song IDs under a new optional key).

## Mac

- **Sidebar**: **Songbook** in Explore (`AppSection.songbook`).
- **`SongbookView`** (new): a grid of **lead-sheet cards** (`TuneCard`): cream-on-ink, five faint staff lines across
  the top, the title in serif italic, composer and year in the label face, and along the bottom a row of dots, one per
  version, placed by year and coloured by era, so the card itself shows how far the tune travelled. Sort by year or
  A–Z; filter by origin. A header line in the style of the other index pages: "40 tunes, 230 versions".
- **`TuneDetailView`** (new):
  - Hero: title in display type over staff lines; "Johnny Green, 1930 · Popular song · AABA, 32 bars".
  - The story (`summary`).
  - **Versions**: a horizontal time axis with the River's era bands faintly behind it and a dot per version. Below
    it, the versions as rows: artwork, artist (linked), year, era chip, the note, duration, play. The landmark rows
    carry a star, as starred tracks do on album pages. *Play in Order* and *Play Radio* buttons.
  - **Also in the Crate**: album cards for other versions on curated records.
  - **Family**: tunes built on this one, or what this is built on.
  - YouTube strip (`VideoStrip`, query: the title plus "jazz live").
- **`TrackRow`**: a small note glyph after a title that's in the Songbook; clicking it opens the tune.
- **`ArtistDetailView`**: a **Songbook** row: tune cards for tunes they wrote, then for versions they own ("their
  *Body and Soul*, 1939").
- **`AlbumDetailView`**: nothing new beyond the tracklist links.
- **`NowPlayingBar`**: with a tune context, the version's note is the "why this track" line.

## Risks

- **Getting the right recording.** An artist may have recorded a tune ten times; the Apple Music match must be the one
  the note describes. The year hint helps; every version's match is printed for review, and playing them is the only
  real check.
- **Availability.** Some landmark versions aren't on Apple Music or sit on compilations with odd titles. Those
  versions still appear, with *Search Music* as the existing unresolved-track fallback does.
- **Writing volume.** Forty stories and around 230 version notes is the largest block of prose in the roadmap. It's
  why package 1 starts with twelve.
- **Opinion presented as fact.** "The version that changed everything" is taste. Notes say what to listen for, which
  is checkable by listening, and leave rankings out.

## Done when

The common checklist, plus: every tune plays at least three versions in order from oldest to newest; no tracklist
links a track to the wrong tune; on the Explore tab, the era carousel and the start of the next row are visible
without scrolling on the smaller iPhone, so it's clear there's more than eras.
