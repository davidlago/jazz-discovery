# 1.6 — Lineages & Glossary

*Released as 1.6.0 on 2026-11-18. As built:*

- *Five trees (trumpet; saxophone as one tree, not split by size; piano; drums; voice), 68 players and 44 lines, each
  line a documented influence (often the later player's own words). Bass, guitar, trombone, clarinet, vibes and
  organ wait: their documented lines in the current cast are too few to draw a tree.*
- *`instruments` on artists isn't needed yet: each tree lists its players explicitly.*
- *The layout places faces by coming of age (born + 20) in lanes chosen near each player's root; tested for no
  overlaps.*
- *35 glossary terms, each with at least one example from tracks.json, without `at`/`until` timings (examples were
  chosen where the thing happens early, and notes say what to wait for). How to Listen runs through So What.*
- *Term links open the term's page rather than a popover. Common words (swing, the head, chorus, changes, break,
  riff, ballad, standard) are `explicitOnly` and never linked automatically.*
- *Not done: excerpt playback (`from`/`until`), the iPhone tree's pinch-zoom canvas and list mode (the tree scrolls
  sideways and "The Line" below it is the list), Lineage stations beyond Play the Lineage.*

How the music works, in two forms. **Lineages** follow one instrument through the century: who learned from whom, and
what changed each time. The **Glossary** explains the words the app already uses ("modal", "comping", "trading
fours") with a sound for each, and makes those words tappable wherever they appear.

Better after 1.1, which adds the instruments the app is short of: it has 21 saxophonists and 19 pianists today, but 3
bassists, 1 organist, and no trombone, vibraphone or flute.

## What people get

- **A family tree per instrument**: trumpet, saxophone, piano, bass, drums, guitar, voice, and smaller ones for
  trombone, clarinet, vibraphone and organ. Faces left to right in time, lines for influence.
- **Play the lineage**: one track per player, in order. Twenty minutes to hear the tenor saxophone change.
- **A glossary of about 50 terms**, each with a one-line definition, a paragraph, and one to three recordings where
  you can hear it.
- **Terms you can tap** in era stories, album notes and tune pages: a dotted underline, a popover with the definition
  and *Hear it*.
- **How to Listen**: a five-step primer for newcomers, built from glossary entries, on what happens in a jazz
  performance.

## Data

### Artist instruments

`Artist.instrument` is one word today ("Saxophone", "Bandleader", "Composer"). Add an optional `instruments` list,
most important first, for people who belong in more than one tree (a multi-instrumentalist; a singing pianist). When
absent, it's `[instrument]`. "Bandleader" and "Composer" stay as roles for the Artists filter and aren't instruments:
those artists name their actual instrument in `instruments` (a bandleader who was a pianist appears in the piano
tree).

### `lineages.json` (new, hand-written)

```json
{
  "lineages": [
    {
      "id": "tenor-saxophone",
      "family": "Saxophone",
      "title": "The Tenor Saxophone",
      "intro": "Two paragraphs on the instrument's role and its big argument (here: two founding sounds).",
      "players": [
        { "artist": "coleman-hawkins", "idea": "What he added, in one sentence.", "track": { "title": "Body and Soul", "artist": "Coleman Hawkins", "year": 1939 } }
      ],
      "links": [
        { "from": "coleman-hawkins", "to": "sonny-rollins", "kind": "influence", "note": "One sentence on what carried over." }
      ]
    }
  ]
}
```

- `players` are artists in the app; each has the one idea they brought and one track that shows it (resolved through
  `tracks.json`).
- `links` have a `kind`: `influence`, `teacher`, `bandmate` (learned on the job) or `reaction` (defined themselves
  against). Each needs a `note`: a line with no reason isn't drawn. This is the difference from Connections, which
  shows who played together; a lineage shows whose sound came from whose.
- Saxophone is split (soprano and alto, tenor, baritone share one page with three bands), because the tenor story and
  the alto story are different arguments. Other families are one tree each.

Influence is interpretation. Every link should be one a standard history would make, most often one the later player
stated themselves, and the note says which. Where it's contested, leave it out.

### `glossary.json` (new, hand-written)

```json
{
  "terms": [
    {
      "id": "trading-fours",
      "term": "Trading fours",
      "also": ["trading eights", "trade fours", "trading"],
      "category": "Form",
      "short": "Soloists take turns playing four bars each, usually with the drummer.",
      "long": "A paragraph.",
      "examples": [
        { "track": { "title": "…", "artist": "…", "year": 1957 }, "at": 312, "until": 372, "note": "What you hear." }
      ],
      "related": ["chorus", "head"]
    }
  ]
}
```

- `category`: Form, Rhythm, Harmony, Sound, Roles, Styles, Slang.
- `also` are other spellings to match in text.
- An example's `at` / `until` are seconds into the recording. They're optional: without them the example plays from
  the start with the note telling you what to wait for. Timings must be found by ear, on the Apple Music version the
  example resolves to; they're added with 1.7's editor, and until then examples are chosen where the thing happens in
  the first half minute, which also makes them work as previews more often.

About 50 terms. A first list: *swing, the head, chorus, changes, comping, walking bass, trading fours, break, stop
time, riff, shout chorus, call and response, blue note, twelve-bar blues, AABA, rhythm changes, contrafact, turnaround,
vamp, ostinato, modal, ballad, double time, rubato, cadenza, block chords, stride, scat, vocalese, mute, growl,
overblowing, sheets of sound, clave, montuno, backbeat, the pocket, groove, front line, rhythm section, sideman,
chart, head arrangement, standard, cutting contest, woodshedding, chops, sitting in, free improvisation, collective
improvisation.*

### `howToListen` (in `glossary.json`)

An ordered list of five steps, each a title, a paragraph and a glossary term: *Find the beat* (swing), *Hear the tune*
(the head), *Follow the solos* (chorus, changes), *Listen to the conversation* (comping, trading fours), *Hear the
tune come back*. One recording runs through all five; 1.7 gives it a full listening guide.

### Validator

Lineage players and link ends are artists in that lineage; no link to oneself; every link has a note; links go forward
in time or are marked `reaction`; each lineage's tracks resolve. Glossary IDs unique, `related` resolve, `also`
spellings don't collide between terms, every term has `short` and at least one example, `at < until` and both within
the track's length.

## Shared code

```swift
struct Lineage: Codable, Identifiable { … }
struct Term: Codable, Identifiable, Hashable { … }

extension JazzLibrary {
    var lineages: [Lineage];  func lineage(_ id: String) -> Lineage?
    func lineages(including artistID: String) -> [Lineage]
    func roots(of artistID: String) -> [LineageLink]        // who they came from
    func heirs(of artistID: String) -> [LineageLink]
    var terms: [Term];  func term(_ id: String) -> Term?
}
```

### Lineage layout

`LineageLayout` in `Shared/Design/Layouts.swift`, next to `GraphLayout`: x is time (coming of age, as Connections
does it), y is a layered tree layout, so that lines read as descent rather than as a web: roots at the vertical
centre, heirs fanning out, crossings minimised by ordering each generation by its parent's position. Deterministic,
tested for no overlapping faces.

### Linking terms in text

`Shared/Model/TermLinker.swift`: given a paragraph, returns an `AttributedString` with glossary links
(`jazzdiscovery://term/<id>`). Rules: match `term` and `also`, case-insensitively, on word boundaries, longest match
first ("rhythm changes" before "changes"); link only the first occurrence of each term per paragraph; never inside a
title or a name (a list of protected strings built from artist, album and tune names: "Giant Steps" isn't "steps",
"Modal Jazz" the era name isn't the term). Common words that are also terms ("break", "standard", "swing", "head",
"chart") only link when a small allow-list of neighbouring words is present or the term is marked `explicitOnly`,
in which case the prose opts in with `[[break]]`. Built once at load for each text and cached.

The views that show long text (`EraDetailView`'s summary, an album's `why`, tune stories, place and label pages) switch
from `Text(String)` to `TermText`, which renders the attributed string and handles the link by showing a popover
instead of opening a URL.

### Player

`play(_ track:, from:, until:, context:)`. With MusicKit, queue entries can carry a start and end time; confirm that
on both platforms, and otherwise start playback and set `playbackTime` (which `seek(to:)` already does), stopping
from the tick. With previews, `from`/`until` are ignored and the popover says "Preview: listen for it" or offers *Open
in Music*.

`PlayContext.term(String)`, `.lineage(String)`.

## Mac

- **Sidebar**: **Lineages** and **Glossary** in Explore.
- **`LineagesView`** (new): one card per instrument, each a row of four or five overlapping duotone faces from its
  tree, the instrument's name in display type, and the count.
- **`LineageDetailView`** (new): intro; then the tree, scrollable sideways with decade ticks along the top as on
  Connections. `GraphNode` is reused for faces. Lines are drawn by kind (solid for influence, double for teacher,
  dashed for reaction). Hovering a face lights its roots and heirs and shows the player's `idea`; hovering a line
  shows its note. Clicking pins the selection and shows a panel with the idea, the track and *Open artist*. *Play the
  Lineage* queues every player's track in order, and the tree follows along, lighting each face as its track plays.
- **`ArtistDetailView`**: a **Lineage** strip: "Came from" faces → this artist → "Led to" faces, with the notes under
  them, linking to the full tree.
- **`GlossaryView`** (new): terms in columns by category, dictionary style: headword in serif bold, the short
  definition, a small play button that plays the first example. A search field filters as you type.
- **Term page**: headword large in serif; the long text; **Hear It** (example rows with the note, the passage's
  times, play); related terms as chips; "Where it shows up": eras and tunes whose text links to it.
- **Term popover** (from `TermText`): headword, short definition, *Hear it*, *More*.
- **How to Listen**: a card at the top of the Glossary and, for new users (nothing heard in the passport yet), on
  Discover. Five numbered steps in the Start Here numeral style, each with its paragraph and a play button.

## iPhone

- **`LineageDetailView`**: the tree pans and pinches like the Connections map. The camera and gestures there live in

  Tapping a face shows a card; tapping a line shows its note. A list mode
  (toggle in the toolbar) shows the same lineage as a vertical chain, which is easier with VoiceOver and large text.
- **Glossary**: an A–Z list with category chips; term pages; the term popover is a small sheet.

## Tests

- `TermLinker`: longest match wins; first occurrence only; nothing inside protected names; `explicitOnly` terms link
  only when opted in; diacritics; no overlapping links. Run over every text in the bundle as a data test: no link
  falls inside an artist, album or tune name.
- `LineageLayout`: deterministic; no two faces overlap; every link's `from` is left of its `to` unless it's a
  reaction.
- `roots` and `heirs` across more than one lineage.
- Excerpt playback stops within a tick of `until` and doesn't scrobble as a full play.

## Risks

- **Influence claims.** The stated rule (a note for every link, sourced to a standard account, omit the contested)
  keeps this honest, and the review of each tree is the most important review in this release.
- **Over-linking.** Dotted underlines everywhere make text tiring. First occurrence per paragraph, the protected
  names, and `explicitOnly` for common words are the controls; the bundle-wide data test catches the worst cases, and
  reading a few pages catches the rest.
- **Examples without timings** are weaker ("listen for it at about five minutes"). Choosing examples where the thing
  happens early is the stopgap until 1.7's editor.
- **Tree layout** for families with many players may need per-lineage hints (a `row` on a player). Allow for it in
  the schema from the start.

## Done when
